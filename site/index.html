Features

Built to stay out of your way, until it shouldn't

Everything about the bar is designed for the middle of a match: you catch it in the corner of your eye, and it never takes anything from your game.

Never in the way

Clicks go straight through the bar, it can never take focus from your game, and it stays out of Alt-Tab. Put it anywhere, even on the taskbar.

Instant, at a glance

Green while you're calm, amber as you get close to your line, red and a little bigger the moment you're over it.

Your last red peak

When you've gone over, a needle and a readout show how far, then clear away after a time you choose. Calm moments are never shown.

A nudge, not a nag

Stay loud for more than a moment and a small breathe card slides in for a few seconds. A laugh doesn't count. It never makes a sound.

Loud the way ears hear it

Levels are A-weighted, like a dB(A) meter, so a high-pitched shout counts as loud as it sounds, not just as loud as its raw energy.

Private by design

No audio is ever recorded or stored, just how loud you were and when. No account, no uploads. Everything stays on your PC.

Up close

It only speaks up when you've been too loud

Talking calmly or getting close, the bar just shows your level. Go over your line and it grows and turns red, a needle marks how far you went, and if you stay loud a breathe card slides in.

  • The readout shows your last red peak, never calm ones
  • It clears itself after 1, 5, 10 or 15 minutes, or whenever you like
  • Or show your calm streak beside the bar instead
Calibrated to you

Your line, from your own voice

Talk normally for ten seconds, then too loudly for five. Vocalm puts your line between the two, through your own microphone, so "too loud" means too loud for you.

  • Measures what Discord actually sends (SteelSeries Sonar or any mic)
  • Never sets your line so close that normal speech trips it
  • Change it any time, against your live voice
The dashboard

Find out when and where you get loud

A dashboard on your own PC shows your voice live, today's loud moments, and the patterns behind them: this week against the last, the hours you get heated, and the games that set you off.

Get started

Up and running in a minute

Download

Get Vocalm-…-windows.zip from the latest release and unzip it anywhere you'll keep it.

Run it

Double-click Vocalm.exe. The bar appears, a tray icon sits by the clock, and the dashboard opens. If Windows says it protected your PC, choose More info, then Run anyway.

Calibrate, then play

In Settings, calibrate your voice (15 seconds), turn on Start with Windows, and play. Games need to run in borderless or windowed mode for any overlay to show.

Privacy

Your voice stays yours

No recordings

Sound becomes a level about 90 times a second and is thrown away straight after.

On your PC

Settings and history live in one folder of yours. The dashboard only answers this PC.

No titles

It notes which game was in front by the program's name, never window titles.

No account

No sign-up, no telemetry, nothing uploaded. Delete one folder and it's gone.

Questions

Good to know

The bar doesn't show over my game

No overlay can draw over a game in exclusive fullscreen. Switch the game to borderless (or windowed) fullscreen in its video settings; it looks the same and the bar shows up.

Will it slow my game down?

No. Vocalm uses a fraction of a percent of your processor and only redraws the bar when your level changes. It never takes focus, so it can't interrupt you.

Which microphone does it listen to?

Whichever you choose in Settings. With SteelSeries Sonar it defaults to Sonar's microphone, which is exactly what Discord sends, so the bar shows what your friends hear. Discord and Vocalm can use the same microphone at the same time.

Does it record what I say?

Never. It keeps how loud you were and when, plus the name of the app in front if you allow it. No audio, no words, no window titles.

Windows says it "protected your PC"

Windows shows this for new apps that haven't paid for a code-signing certificate. Choose More info, then Run anyway; you only need to once. Only download Vocalm from its GitHub releases.
